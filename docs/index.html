A theory of pair bonding, written as an operating system

Everything settles at 0.618

Regulus is an attempt to write down what happens between two people using the same mathematics an engineer uses to keep a chemical plant from exploding. It sounds absurd until you follow it, and then the awkward part is how much of it is measurable. The next five paragraphs are the entire thing in plain words; everything after them is just how it works.

Thomas, who wrote the framework, named this version himself: "Regulus 4 Retards / R4R." His word, about his own work.

The whole thing, before any maths

Five paragraphs. No symbols, no jargon. If you read only this much you will have the framework — everything further down is the machinery underneath it.

One. Your body is a factory with floors. The gut is the ground floor, the thinking part is near the top. Sensation travels up, decisions travel down. The surprising bit is that the ground floor can overrule the top — which is why you cannot think your way out of feeling sick.

Two. When the thinking floor cannot put a name to a feeling, that feeling does not disappear. It travels back down and lands in your gut. Unnamed emotion becomes stomach ache. That is the founding observation, and it is the least controversial thing in the whole framework.

Three. Anything that has to remember and forget at the same time settles at the same balance: keep about 62%, release about 38%. Hold on to everything and you are overwhelmed; hold on to nothing and there is nothing there. That single ratio is what the rest of the document is built around.

Four. Here is what Regulus adds. Two people who bond do not merely end up near each other — they change each other, and past a certain point you cannot get the earlier version of yourself back. The framework treats that as a physical event with a physical signature: the arteries feeding your heart relax. Not as a metaphor. The organ that feeds everything else finally lets itself be fed.

Five. And the useful part. Something can look exactly like love from the outside and be its opposite. The same behaviours — the obsession, the constant checking, the inability to leave. A completely different body: heart shut rather than open, stress hormones climbing rather than falling. So the framework hands you one question to tell them apart.

Who in this system is unchanged? §9.2 · the whole diagnostic, in six words

If both of you have been changed by it, that is a bond. If only one of you is being changed while the other stays exactly as they were, that is extraction wearing a bond's clothes. The same test works on cults, on propaganda, and on addiction — where the substance never changes and only the person does.

You can check it for about thirty pounds, which is the part that lifts this out of philosophy. A saliva cortisol test, or a heart-rate-variability strap. Does being near this person calm your nervous system down, or wind it up? You cannot fake that number.

That is the framework. Everything below is how it gets there — and the diagnostic itself is further down, as seven questions you can answer.

§3 · Convergence constant

Start anywhere. You end up in the same place.

Regulus opens with one equation and a claim about it. The equation describes how a quantity it calls κ ("kappa") changes over time. You can read κ as how much of what arrives a system holds on to — a memory that keeps everything is useless, and so is one that keeps nothing.

The claim is that κ has one stable resting point, and it is 1/φ = 0.618033… — one divided by the golden ratio. Drag the starting value anywhere in the panel. It falls to the same place every time.

κ convergence · ∂κ/∂t = α(1/φ−κ)(1−κ)κ + γξ(t) κ = 0.6180

Why this number and not 0.6

Because 0.618… is the only number that is its own recursion. Each line below is a separate property, and one number satisfies all of them at once:

Properties satisfied simultaneously by κ* = 1/φ
IdentityReads as
φ · κ* = 1its product with the golden ratio is exactly one
κ* = φ − 1it is the golden ratio with the whole part removed
κ*² = 1 − κ*squaring it gives you exactly what was left over
κ*ⁿ = κ*ⁿ⁻² − κ*ⁿ⁻¹Fibonacci, living in the exponents [corrected]
κ*/(1−κ*) = φwhat you keep, over what you drop, is φ

That last one has a concrete meaning. For anything that must remember and forget at the same time — a queue, a buffer, a person — the balance that works out algebraically is keep 61.8%, release 38.2%.

Three fixed points

κ = 0 — unstable. Nothing is retained. The void.

κ = 1/φ — stable. Everything nearby falls here.

κ = 1 — unstable. Everything is retained. Pure noise.

Constants, none hand-tuned
α3.618…= φ + 2
β0.2360…= κ*³
γ0.0902…= κ*⁵
Emin0.1180…= 1/2φ³
|δΨcrit|0.3820…= κ*²

Every constant is a power or sum of φ. The system tunes by φ, not to it.

§2, §4 · Spine and levels

A person, drawn as a factory

ASS-OS — the framework Regulus extends — borrows its shape from ISA-95, the standard that describes how an industrial plant is layered from the physical floor up to the boardroom. The claim is not that people resemble factories. It is that both are control systems, and control systems fail in the same small number of ways.

The prime spine

Underneath the levels sits a counting sequence: apply "take the next prime" to itself, over and over, starting at 1. You get 1, 2, 3, 5, 11, 31, 127, 709. The gaps widen faster than exponentially, and Regulus uses that acceleration as the skeleton for seven levels of awareness — each level costing dramatically more than the one below it.

Treat this as structure the author chose, not structure anyone measured. It is elegant. Elegance is not evidence, and the document says so itself.

§7 · Love equations

Seven equations for what happens between two people

This is the part that makes Regulus more than a rewrite of ASS-OS. Seven equations, each one a phase — from the first flicker of recognition to the thing that stays after someone has gone. The document is careful about its own status here, calling them "formal metaphor that encodes subjective experience in mathematical structure." Open each one.

7.1RecognitionR = ∫ ΨA·ΨB dV

How much two people's inner shapes overlap. If you process the world in a similar way, the number is large. If you are genuinely orthogonal, it is zero — and no amount of proximity will produce anything.

The document's own gloss is the best line in the section: the integral was always computable, you just hadn't run the calculation until she showed up and the product came back non-zero. Recognition comes first and decides everything after it.

7.2AttractionF = −R · κ*/r

Recognition, divided by distance. Note it is 1/r, not 1/r² — the framework's reason is that its proposed channel is a one-dimensional waveguide, a line rather than a volume.

The consequence is the interesting bit: with no recognition there is no attraction at any distance, and with high recognition there is attraction at any distance.

7.3Resonanceω± = √(ω² ± kc)

Couple two oscillators and you always get two modes, never one: an in-phase mode (harmony) and an antiphase mode (discord).

So the claim is structural rather than sentimental. Love is not the absence of the discord mode. Both modes exist in any coupled system; the bond is stable when it can hold both at once. A relationship with no antiphase mode isn't harmonious — it isn't coupled.

7.4VulnerabilityΨ(seen) = Ψ(alone) + δΨ

Let someone in and their imprint is added to your state. Above a threshold this is irreversible: you do not get Ψ(alone) back.

Three failure modes fall straight out. Walls are forcing δΨ = 0. The mask is presenting a false Ψ so nothing can couple to the real one. And maintaining a mask costs entropy continuously — the document puts the drop at ΔH = −0.5 the moment it comes off, the single largest entropy reduction available in a pair bond.

7.5Pair entropyI(A;B) = H(A) + H(B) − H(A,B) > 0

A bonded pair is measurably more ordered than the two people apart. That is what strictly positive mutual information means.

The framing is unembarrassed: the second law says entropy increases; love says not here it doesn't. A local eddy of order, and — the part people skip — one that runs on continuous energy input. Attention, presence, care. Stop supplying it and entropy resumes. The bond requires maintenance, not just establishment.

7.6Speed of lovev = √(kc/φ)

Three regimes, depending on how strongly the two are coupled.

Below φ, love is slower than understanding: you know them first, feeling follows. At exactly φ, the two arrive together — the instant lock. Above φ, the feeling outruns comprehension: the body knows before the mind does, and the feeling arrives before you have a framework to hold it.

7.7Persistence∂(δΨ)/∂r = ∂(δΨ)/∂t = 0

The imprint does not decay with distance or with time — and explicitly not because a signal is passing between the two people.

The analogy the document uses is exact and does real work: two tuning forks cut to the same pitch need no wire between them. They resonate because they are the same shape. The contact was the tuning. The resonance is what remains.

§8 · Binding operator Ŝ

Ŝ — the point of no return

Ŝ ("Shekinah", a Kabbalistic word for indwelling) is the operator that takes two separate states and produces one joined state that cannot be separated back. Formally: remove B and you do not recover the original A. A has been permanently modified.

There is a threshold, and it is the same constant again: |δΨcrit| = κ*² = 0.382. Below it, imprints fade — acquaintances, passing encounters. Above it, they don't. And κ* + κ*² = 1 exactly, so crossing the threshold means the imprint fills precisely the space that was empty. Nothing gets displaced.

The energy claim is counter-intuitive and testable in principle: binding costs 11.8% of capacity and frees the rest. The bound state is cheaper to maintain than two independent ones — which the document offers as the reason pair-bonded people report feeling more capable rather than less.

Authentic binding requires both fields to change. One-directional modification is not love — it is the other operator. §8.5 · Bilateral requirement

§8.4 · Claimed physical substrate

Regulus does not leave Ŝ abstract. It names a specific event: coronary vasodilation — the arteries feeding the heart widening under parasympathetic input. The organ that feeds everything else finally letting itself be fed.

Seven correlates it calls unfakeable:

  1. pupil dilation viewing partner
  2. skin perfusion rise — the "glow"
  3. voice fundamental frequency shift
  4. iris warming in hazel eyes
  5. hands warm and dry
  6. blink rate normalising to 8–12/min
  7. breath deepening toward 6/min

Its own supporting argument runs backwards through Takotsubo cardiomyopathy: grief visibly deforms the left ventricle on an echocardiogram. If the heart can change shape from loss, the reverse direction is at least not absurd.

§9 · Mimic operator M̂

M̂ — and the question that costs thirty pounds to answer

This is the sharpest thing in the document, and the reason it is worth anyone's time. M̂ is coercion wearing love's behaviour. Obsession, constant monitoring, inability to leave — from outside, identical. The physiology is the opposite: Ŝ opens the heart, M̂ runs the dependency circuit while the heart stays shut.

Run it on a bond you're thinking about

Seven rows, straight from §9. Answer honestly — nothing is transmitted anywhere, this all runs in your browser.

Near this person, does your chest feel warm and open, or tight and guarded? Ŝ: coronary vasodilation · M̂: constriction
Have both of you been changed by this, or only one of you? The single metric — who in this system is unchanged?
Is each of you more functional than before, or less? Ŝ: H(pair) < H(alone) for both · M̂: target degrades
In their company does your nervous system get calmer or more vigilant? Saliva cortisol or an HRV strap — about £30
When the mask came off, did you choose to show it, or was it taken? Ŝ: seen · M̂: exposed
Remove every constraint keeping you here. Does the bond survive? Ŝ persists in freedom · M̂ requires captivity
Did this build gradually, or did it feel manufactured and fast? Organic v_love versus forced acceleration

This is a framework's self-check, not a clinical instrument, and no one should end or salvage a relationship on the strength of seven buttons on a web page. What it is good for is naming a thing you had already noticed. If the answers frighten you, that is worth taking to a person, not a browser.

The claim that makes it fall over, if it's wrong

§9.3 argues that once someone has felt authentic binding, the mimic becomes permanently detectable — you cannot unfeel what open felt like, and closed-pretending-to-be-open can't reproduce the actual vasodilation. That is a real, falsifiable prediction. It says people with a prior secure bond should detect coercive dynamics faster than people without one. Nobody has run it.

Two transcription errors, found by implementing it

Neither is a flaw in the thinking. Both are the kind of slip that only surfaces when somebody tries to run the equations, and both will stop the next person who tries. The short version is the table below. The full working — the arithmetic, the Python you can re-run, the stability argument — lives in ERRATA.md at the root of this repository, deliberately kept off this page.

§As printedShould beHow it shows
3.1 κ*ⁿ = κ*ⁿ⁻¹ − κ*ⁿ⁻² κ*ⁿ = κ*ⁿ⁻² − κ*ⁿ⁻¹ Every answer comes out the right size with a minus sign wrongly in front of it — −0.236068 where the paper's own next section says +0.2360. A document contradicting itself on one line is how you know it is a slip of the pen.
3.3 ∂κ/∂t = α(κ − 1/φ)(1−κ)κ ∂κ/∂t = α(1/φ − κ)(1−κ)κ As printed, the equation does the opposite of what the paragraph beside it says. Nudge the value up off 0.618 and it runs further away instead of settling back. Swap the two terms in the first bracket and every claim the section makes comes true. The paper's own stability working agrees, which is the strongest sign the thinking was right and only the sign slipped.

The live instrument at the top of this page runs the corrected §3.3 equation, and the identity table uses the corrected §3.1 order. Both are flagged here rather than quietly fixed.

The strongest objection, stated properly

Picture a ruler marked only in whole centimetres, and someone using it to prove that two things differ in length by about a millimetre and a half. They might be right. The ruler cannot say. That is the exact state of the evidence for 0.618: the difference being claimed is about seven times smaller than the smallest thing the measurement can see.

The same sentence with the decimals put back. HeartMath's coherence window is roughly 0.60–0.65. That window contains 1/φ = 0.6180, and it also contains 5/8 = 0.625, and plain 0.6. The gap between the first two is 0.0070, against a window 0.05 wide. No measurement currently offered can tell them apart — so at present φ is chosen, not confirmed. The appraisal in this repository scores that specific claim 8/10 on a bullshit meter for exactly this reason, and the framework is better off for having the number stated than defended.

None of which touches §7 and §9, which is where the value actually sits. You can throw the golden ratio out entirely and the bilateral-modification criterion still stands on its own.
